# E9 - H5-H7: parking enforcement, staggered departures, decentralized core demand

- **Status:** done — one rejected (H5), two supported (H6, H7, with a caveat on H7)
- **Owner:** human
- **Tests hypothesis:** H5, H6, H7
- **Prediction (written BEFORE running):**
  - H5 (parking, free-flow 30->35 km/h): if real, the paired-seed CI excludes zero, negative (less delay), most/all seeds agreeing in sign.
  - H6 (staggered, demand window 3600->5400s): if real, delay drops because the same total demand is spread thinner at any instant - but if this is mostly a survivorship-bias artifact (more time to drain, not less actual congestion), completion should rise alongside it, which needs checking before trusting the raw number (per D11/D16's established pattern).
  - H7 (decentralized, -11.4% total demand to core zones): expect a real reduction, since this is partly just "less total traffic" - the honest reading has to separate "decentralization works" from "we deleted 11% of trips," which a pure volume-reduction control would need to fully isolate (not run here - time-boxed).
  - All three falsified if their respective paired-seed 95% CI includes zero.
- **Method:** `scripts/compare_extra.py`, 15 seeds, tmax=14400, fixed-time signals throughout, each config isolated (not stacked with H1-H4's levers).
- **Assumptions relied on:** A3 (free-flow speed, H5 directly tests a partial reversal of it); new assumption for H6 (staggering modeled as a wider demand window, not real schedule data - [SURVEY]); new assumption for H7 (25% core-attraction cut, round-number scenario, not a measured relocation plan - [SURVEY], same status as A1/A9).
- **Budget:** 60 runs (4 configs x 15 seeds), 3 at a time, ~20-25 min.
- **Code & command:** `python scripts/build_od_decentralized.py` then `python scripts/compare_extra.py --seeds 15 --jobs 3`
- **Results:** `results/extra.partial.csv` (60 runs: 15 baseline + 15 each of parking/staggered/decentralized, tmax=14400). Checked completion before trusting any raw number, per this project's established practice (D11, D16):

  | Config | Raw paired delay | Completion (mean / min) | Clean-seed-only paired delay |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | parking (free-flow 30→35) | -28.61% ± 11.68% (sig.) | 88.4% / 79.8% | **-1.71% ± 13.47%, NOT significant** |
  | staggered (window 3600→5400s) | -38.34% ± 7.03% (sig.) | 99.2% / 88.6% | -36.33% ± 6.99%, **SIGNIFICANT**, 12/12 |
  | decentralized (-11.4% core demand) | -33.83% ± 5.62% (sig.) | 99.4% / 91.5% | -31.13% ± 4.57%, **SIGNIFICANT**, 12/12 |

- **Interpretation:**
  - **H5 rejected.** The raw -28.6% is almost entirely a completion-bias artifact: 10 of 15 seeds dropped below 100% completion (one as low as 79.8%) under the higher free-flow speed. Among the 5 seeds unaffected by this, the real effect collapses to -1.7%, not significant. This is a second confirmation of E7's capacity-cliff finding on the free_flow_kmh knob: E7 found instability at -20% (24 km/h); this finds it again at a *more free-flowing* +17% (35 km/h) in the opposite direction. The network appears to sit near a knife-edge on this parameter in both directions, not just toward congestion - plausibly because changing free-flow speed shifts DUO's relative route-time landscape enough to concentrate traffic onto different routes than the calibration assumed, overloading a different junction than before. Raising free-flow speed alone (representing partial parking/encroachment removal) is **not a reliable delay-reduction lever** in this model as implemented.
  - **H6 supported, and the strongest single-lever result in this project.** -36.33% survives the completion check cleanly (only 1/15 seeds affected, and removing it barely changes the number). Honest caveat: a 50%-longer demand window is a *large* staggering ask (effectively ~30 minutes of schedule spread across many institutions), and part of the mechanical effect is simply "less demand loaded per unit time" - a true measure of staggering's benefit would need to model realistic, smaller schedule shifts (e.g. 15 min) to know if the benefit scales down proportionally or is mostly front-loaded.
  - **H7 supported, but confounded.** -31.13% survives the completion check too. But this demand variant cuts total trips by 11.4% as well as redistributing them - the result cannot yet be cleanly attributed to "decentralization helps" vs. "there are just fewer trips." A volume-matched control (same -11.4% cut, applied uniformly rather than core-targeted) would isolate the two effects; not run here, time-boxed.
- **Next:** For the pitch: H6 (staggered departures) is now the single strongest tested result in the project and arguably deserves equal billing with signal optimization, though it's software/policy-only too (schedule coordination, not infrastructure) - unlike signals, it needs behavior change from many institutions, which carries its own (different) adoption risk, not an enforcement-gaming one. H7 needs the volume-matched control before its effect size is quoted as "decentralization," not just "less demand." H5 is a real negative result worth reporting with the same honesty as H4/E8's tidal-flow finding - raising free-flow speed is not a lever to recommend.
