# E6 - GEH validation against observed traffic counts: feasibility and plan

- **Status:** done — result is a genuine validation failure, not a transcription gap (see below)
- **Owner:** human (data transcription step cannot be automated reliably — see below)
- **Tests hypothesis:** none directly — this validates the baseline model (E1) and, by extension, every downstream comparison (H1-H3), against reality rather than only against itself
- **Prediction (written BEFORE running):** If the calibrated baseline is a reasonable representation of Kathmandu's arterial network, GEH < 5 on a majority of the locations where both a model link and an observed count exist (README's own stated target: ≥85% of links). If GEH is high everywhere, the baseline's absolute numbers (not necessarily its *relative* lever comparisons) are not trustworthy as a description of the city.

## Data source found (feasible, with real caveats)

**JICA / Department of Roads (2012). "Data Collection Survey on Traffic Improvement in Kathmandu Valley." Final Report, October 2012.** Publicly hosted: https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12082459_01.pdf (main volume) and `_02` through `_06` (chapters/appendices). Confirmed by direct text extraction (pdftotext on the fetched PDFs — not from a search snippet), not just a citation:

- **44 roadside traffic-count points** (24hr or 16hr counts, by 11 vehicle types), full location list in Table 5.3.7 (e.g. Thankot, Kalankisthan, Balkhu, Koteshwor-to-Bhaktapur, Chabahil-to-Bauddha, Ring Road segments).
- **10 screen-line count points** (river-crossing counts, 24hr) at named bridges.
- **10 major-intersection turning-movement counts**, each 1hr AM peak + 1hr PM peak: **Kalankisthan, Kalimati, Sorhakhutte, Balaju, Thapathali, Old Baneshwor, New Baneshwor, Koteshwor, Chabahil, Gausala.** Five of these (Kalanki/Kalankisthan, Thapathali, Koteshwor, Chabahil) are the **exact same landmarks** `validate_hotspots.py` already checks face-validity against — this is not a coincidence to engineer, it's already aligned.
- A 1991-vs-2011 comparison table and vehicle-type growth ratios (e.g. motorcycle growth ~19.8x from 1991, average ~16%/year) that could support extrapolating 2011 counts toward a more current estimate if needed.

## Feasibility problems found (real, checked directly — not assumed)

1. **The actual numeric tables are scanned images, not text**, in the PDF appendix (`12082459_04.pdf`, Appendix 1.2.1 "Traffic Volume at Survey Points" and 1.2.3 "Traffic Count at Major Intersection"). Confirmed directly: `pdftotext` extracts the surrounding labels and headers cleanly (e.g. "A. Kalankisthan Intersection (9:00-10:00)") but returns nothing where the actual count numbers should be — those cells are embedded JPEG2000 images (~993×129px each, consistent with scanned table rows). **This means no numbers can be pulled automatically; each table needs a human to look at the image and transcribe it.** For 10 intersections × 2 time periods × multiple approaches/vehicle types, this is maybe 1-2 hours of careful manual work, not a script.
2. **The survey is from December 2011-January 2012** (adjusted for a fuel-shortage anomaly that hit mid-survey — the report's own Table 5.3.8 gives correction ratios by vehicle type, which is actually a sign of a careful survey, not a red flag). That's **~14-15 years old relative to this project's 2026 OSM snapshot and current-state claims.** Any GEH comparison needs an explicit caveat: this validates "is the model's *structure/relative pattern* plausible," not "does the model match 2026 Kathmandu." The growth-ratio tables in the same report could extrapolate 2011 counts forward, but that compounds another assumption on top of the comparison and should be reported separately, not folded in silently.
3. **Vehicle-type and time granularity need conversion before comparing:** the survey counts 11 raw vehicle types over 1hr AM/PM peaks (intersections) or 16/24hr totals (roadside/screen-line); this project's model outputs PCU-weighted peak-hour link volumes. Converting the survey's own raw counts to PCU is straightforward (the report gives vehicle categories; this project's own A2 PCU weights could apply, or the report may have its own PCU factors in a section not yet checked) — but it's a real step, not a given.
4. **Coverage is partial, not comprehensive:** ~44+10+10 = ~64 named locations vs. this project's 590 links. GEH would be computed at the subset of locations where a model link can be identified as corresponding to a named survey point (a manual or semi-manual matching step, similar to `nearest_node()` in `run_baseline.py` but for named roads, not zone centroids) — not network-wide. The README's "≥85% of links" target needs rewording to "≥85% of the matched validation locations," which is a smaller and more honest claim.
5. **Checked (2026-10-17):** the 2017 JICA follow-up — *"The Project on Urban Transport Improvement for Kathmandu Valley," Final Report Vol. II, May 2017* (JICA + DoR + Kathmandu Valley Development Authority, report code 17-068, https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12289682_01.pdf) — does **not** contain fresher raw counts; it explicitly states its intersection saturation analysis is "based on the traffic counting survey by JICA in 2012" (same source as above, not new data). **However**, it's a genuine upgrade for feasibility: unlike the 2012 report's scanned-image tables, this volume presents **clean, machine-readable text tables** (confirmed via direct `pdftotext` extraction, not a snippet) with peak-hour volumes already in the right units:
   - **Table 8.2.5** (vehicle/h, AM/PM peak + average): New Baneshwor (9,123 avg), Thapathali (7,395 avg), Sorakhutte (4,395 avg), Old Baneshwor (3,910 avg)
   - **Table 8.2.6** (pcu/h peak + saturation rate — already PCU-converted, matching this project's own unit): Kalimati (4,897 pcu/h, sat. 0.84), **Koteshwor (9,996 pcu/h, sat. 0.97)**
   - **Checked and confirmed missing from this volume:** Kalanki, Chabahil, Gausala, Balaju — these appear only in **Figure 8.2.11** (a saturation-ratio chart, i.e. an image, not a text table) in this volume. They may be in another volume of the same 2017 report (Vol. I or Vol. III appendices, not yet checked) or require the manual transcription route already planned for the 2012 report.
   - **Net effect:** Koteshwor and Thapathali (2 of the 5 landmarks matching `validate_hotspots.py`) now need **zero manual transcription** — real numbers in hand already, reproduced above. 4 bonus intersections (New Baneshwor, Sorakhutte, Old Baneshwor, Kalimati) are available too, usable to extend the hotspot landmark list if useful. Kalanki, Chabahil, Gausala, Balaju still need the manual route (or checking the other 2017 volumes).

## Assumptions this plan would rely on

- A new assumption (not yet in `assumptions.md` — add on acceptance): manual transcription of scanned count tables is accurate (no digit-transcription errors) — mitigated by having two people/passes check high-stakes numbers, or at minimum spot-checking a sample.
- A11 (to register): 2011-2012 survey counts are an adequate proxy for the pattern (not the level) of current congestion, given ~14 years of growth — supported partially by the report's own 1991-2011 growth-ratio tables, which could be used to flag this rather than ignore it.

## First result (2026-10-17, `scripts/geh_check.py`)

Ran GEH on the 2 free landmarks. **Mixed, not a clean pass:**

| Landmark | Model (pcu/h) | Observed (pcu/h) | GEH | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thapathali | 4,570 | 4,622 (7,395 veh/h x 0.625 PCU) | 0.77 | good fit (<5) |
| Koteshwor | 2,060 | 9,996 | **102.22** | **poor fit, not close** |

Koteshwor's node match is precise (0.03 km — not a landmark-mismatch artifact), so this is a real discrepancy: the model undershoots observed volume by ~5x at Koteshwor specifically. This is informative, not just a failure to patch: the baseline's calibration (A3/A4) fixes mean speed as a single aggregate target, which pins total demand *level* but puts no constraint on how demand *distributes* across specific links/intersections. Thapathali landing close and Koteshwor landing far apart, on the same calibration, is consistent with exactly that gap — the model can match the city on average while being badly wrong at a specific, real bottleneck. Logged as D8 in `decisions.md`.

This changes the framing of E6's remaining work: it's not just "transcribe more data to raise coverage," it's "find out whether Koteshwor is a one-off (demand from outside the study box again, as with Kalanki/E5) or a sign the model's spatial distribution of demand needs rework before it can carry a validation claim at all."

## Extended result (6 landmarks, using 4 more already in hand from the 2017 report — no transcription)

| Landmark | Model (pcu/h) | Observed (pcu/h) | GEH | Verdict | Coords |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thapathali | 4,570 | 4,622 | 0.77 | good | surveyed landmark |
| Old Baneshwor | 2,290 | 2,444 | 3.16 | good | approx (OSM geocoded) |
| Sorakhutte | 3,660 | 2,747 | 16.13 | **poor (overshoot)** | approx |
| Kalimati | 3,740 | 4,897 | 17.61 | **poor (undershoot)** | approx |
| New Baneshwor | 4,010 | 5,702 | 24.28 | **poor (undershoot)** | approx |
| Koteshwor | 2,060 | 9,996 | 102.22 | **poor (undershoot)** | surveyed landmark |

**2/6 (33%) pass GEH<5 — far short of the README's own ≥85% target.** This rules out the hopeful "Koteshwor is a one-off" reading from the first result. It also rules out a clean "big convergence points always undershoot" story: Sorakhutte is also a major multi-road convergence point (Thamel/Swayambhunath/Balaju) but the model *overshoots* there, in the opposite direction from Koteshwor/Kalimati/New Baneshwor. Coordinate precision doesn't explain the pattern either — the two surveyed-precision landmarks (Thapathali, Koteshwor) split exactly as evenly as the four approximate ones (1 good, 1 bad vs. 1 good, 3 bad).

**Honest reading:** this is a real validation failure at the intersection level, not a coverage gap waiting on more transcription. The baseline's single mean-speed calibration can match the city on average while being wrong — in both directions — at specific, real intersections. This is consistent with, and sharpens, the calibration-identifiability limitation already on record (A4): matching one aggregate number constrains nothing about spatial distribution, and this result shows that gap is large in practice, not just a theoretical risk.

## Next

1. **Done:** ran GEH on Koteshwor and Thapathali — see result above. Not the clean proof hoped for; Koteshwor is a real problem to explain, not just more data to gather.
2. **Checked (2026-10-17):** ruled out both cheap structural explanations.
   - **Not a map-boundary issue like Kalanki (E5):** Koteshwor is 4.22 km from the east edge and 4.88 km from the south edge of the OSM extract — nowhere near the boundary. Confirmed by direct distance calculation, not assumed.
   - **Not a missing-geometry issue:** the matched model node (6275508137) has 7 links including both Araniko Highway and Kathmandu Ring Road — 4 inbound approaches with reasonable lane counts (2-4 lanes each). The network correctly represents Koteshwor as a major convergence point. Confirmed by reading `data/network_links.csv` directly.
   - **Remaining explanation:** the calibration gap already on record (A4) — a single city-wide mean-speed target fixes total demand level but constrains nothing about where it concentrates. A real convergence point like Koteshwor can carry disproportionate volume that a uniform 30%-arterial-share scaling doesn't reproduce, while a more "typical" arterial link (Thapathali) fits fine under the same global scalar. This is a genuine finding about the calibration's limits, not a bug to patch.
3. **Done:** extended to 6 landmarks using data already in hand (no transcription) — see "Extended result" above. **2/6 pass, confirming this is a real, systematic spatial-distribution problem, not an artifact of one unlucky point.** More transcription at this point would most likely add more failures to document, not raise the pass rate — the honest move now is to decide how to handle this finding, not to gather more data hoping for a better average.
4. **Human decision needed, now sharper than before:** three options —
   (a) Report this validation result as-is in the paper: the model matches city-wide mean speed but fails intersection-level GEH validation (2/6, 33%) — a real, stated limitation on how much weight the absolute delay numbers (not necessarily the relative lever comparisons in H1-H3) can bear.
   (b) Attempt a second-stage calibration that fits multiple intersections simultaneously (e.g. per-zone or per-corridor demand scaling instead of one global scalar) — this is a real modeling effort, not a quick fix, and risks overfitting to these 6 points.
   (c) Reframe what GEH validation is *for* in this paper: use it to validate the *relative* claims (does H1-H3's pattern of improvement hold at individual intersections too, not just in aggregate) rather than the baseline's absolute volumes — this sidesteps the spatial-calibration gap rather than closing it.
