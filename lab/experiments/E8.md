# E8 - Tidal-flow lever (H4): paired-seed test under AM-biased demand

- **Status:** done — H4 rejected as implemented, and the reason is the finding
- **Owner:** human
- **Tests hypothesis:** H4
- **Prediction (written BEFORE running the paired sweep):** A single-seed check found tidal vs no-tidal delay differ by −2.3% (2250.6s -> 2198.6s) under AM demand. That's the *same magnitude* as this project's documented seed-noise sensitivity (a 0.02% demand change once moved delay 2.3%), so it cannot be trusted alone. If H4 holds, the paired-seed 95% CI across 15 seeds should exclude zero on the negative side, with most/all seeds agreeing in sign (matching H1-H3's pattern of 15/15 negative). If the CI straddles zero, or seeds split roughly evenly between positive and negative, the −2.3% single-seed result was noise, not a real effect, and H4 is not supported with only 3 corridors / 1 lane-equivalent shifted.
- **Method:** `scripts/build_od_directional.py` (AM demand: core-bound trips x1.4, core-outbound x1/1.4, core = closest 20% of zones to the mass-weighted centroid). `scripts/tidal_lanes.py` (3 radial corridors, >=2 lanes/direction, shift 1 lane-equivalent of capacity from light to heavy direction). `scripts/compare_tidal.py`: 15 seeds, fixed-time signals in both configs, isolating the tidal lever alone (not stacked with H1-H3's levers yet).
- **Metric & baselines:** paired difference in average delay (s), AM demand, no_tidal vs tidal, 95% t-CI, same methodology as E2-E4.
- **Assumptions relied on:** new assumption to register - AM/PM directional-bias factor (1.4x) is a guess, no Kathmandu commute-direction survey found (same `[SURVEY]`-tagged status as A1/A9); corridor choice (3 named roads) picked by radial-distance heuristic, not a traffic survey of where tidal flow would actually be sited.
- **Budget:** 30 runs (2 configs x 15 seeds), 3 at a time, ~15-20 min.
- **Code & command:** `python scripts/compare_tidal.py --seeds 15 --jobs 3`
- **Results:** `results/tidal.csv`, `results/tidal_run.log`. Raw paired result: delay −220.6s (−9.53% ± 10.47%), 8/15 seeds negative, **not significant by the CI alone** — but checking completion revealed something worse than noise:

  | | no_tidal | tidal |
  |---|---|---|
  | Seeds at 100% completion | 12/15 | 7/15 |
  | Seeds that dropped below 100% *because of the lever* (were 100% without it) | - | **5/15** (seeds 5, 7, 8, 13, 14) |

  Decomposed: among the 7 seeds with 100% completion in *both* configs (the only genuinely comparable ones), the lever makes delay **worse on average** (+37.1s, only 3/7 improved). The apparent −9.53% headline number is driven almost entirely by the 5 seeds where the lever caused *new* gridlock that wasn't there without it — exactly the survivorship-bias artifact this project has hit before (D11, the original signal-placement trap): fewer trips finish, so average delay among survivors looks better while the network is actually worse.

- **Interpretation:** **H4 is rejected for this implementation**, and not just by "CI includes zero" — the honest finding is that shifting 1 full lane-equivalent of capacity off the light direction on 3 corridors is *too aggressive*: it creates new bottlenecks on the lightened direction that didn't exist before, for 1/3 of seeds. The core tidal-flow *idea* isn't disproven — this specific parameterization is. A real tidal-flow design would need either a smaller shift (e.g. 0.5 lane-equivalents instead of 1.0), corridors chosen by checking the light direction actually has slack capacity to give up (not just picked by radial-distance heuristic), or both. This is a genuine negative result, same category as E5's cordon-demand finding — informative, not a failure to report.
- **Next:** If pursuing this further: (1) re-run with a smaller lane_shift (0.5) to check whether the gridlock side-effect was simply over-aggressive, not a fundamental flaw in the idea; (2) check each corridor's *light*-direction baseline V/C before applying the shift, so capacity is only taken from directions that can actually spare it. For the hackathon pitch specifically: report this as the honest result of testing the idea rigorously — "we built it, tested it statistically, and found the naive version backfires by over-starving the off-peak direction" is a stronger, more credible proof point than silently dropping a result that didn't work.

## Follow-up: gentler shift (0.5 lane-equivalents instead of 1.0)

- **Prediction (written BEFORE running):** If the gridlock side-effect was purely from over-aggression (taking too much capacity off the light direction), halving the shift to 0.5 lane-equivalents should eliminate or sharply reduce the number of seeds that newly gridlock (down from 5/15), and the "clean" (100%-completion-both) seeds should show a smaller magnitude of harm than the +37.1s average seen at shift=1.0 - possibly flipping to a real, if small, improvement. If seeds still newly gridlock at half the shift, the problem is the corridor choice (no slack on the light direction at all), not just the magnitude, and H4 needs capacity-aware corridor selection instead, not just a gentler dial.
- **Method:** Same as above, `--lane-shift 0.5`.
- **Results:** (pending)
- **Interpretation:** (pending)

- **Results (gentler shift):** `results/tidal_half.csv`. Gridlock side-effect eliminated: 0/15 seeds newly gridlocked (down from 5/15 at full shift). But among the 12 clean (100%-completion-both) seeds, delay got *worse* on average (+86.9s, only 3/12 improved - a lower success rate than the 3/7 at full shift). Raw paired result: +63.2s (+2.76% +/- 5.12%), not significant.
- **Interpretation: prediction half-confirmed.** The gridlock side-effect was indeed just over-aggression (confirmed - fixed by halving the shift), but the underlying "does it help" question gets a clearer no, not a reveal of a hidden benefit. This points at the corridor-selection half of the original prediction: these 3 corridors (picked by a radial-distance heuristic, not by checking whether the light direction actually has slack capacity to give up) are the wrong corridors, not just the wrong magnitude. Taking capacity from a direction that still needs it hurts regardless of how much you take.
- **Next:** H4 is rejected for both parameterizations tried. A real next attempt would need to check each corridor's *light*-direction baseline V/C before applying any shift - only reallocate from a direction with genuine slack. Not attempted here given time; reporting the two honest negative results (D16, D17) is the right stopping point for the hackathon pitch unless there's a specific reason to keep iterating on corridor selection.
