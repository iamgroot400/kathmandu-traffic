# E3 - Congestion pricing vs fixed-time

- **Status:** done
- **Owner:** human
- **Tests hypothesis:** H2
- **Prediction (written BEFORE running):** If H2 holds we expect pricing to reduce total delay relative to fixed-time (via rerouting away from tolled links), CI excluding zero. If the distribution of per-seed delays is strongly bimodal, that would suggest the toll pushes the network between two qualitatively different routing regimes rather than smoothly reducing load.
- **Method:** Same network/demand/routing as E1, links tolled at 0.2× their observed delay (schedule from a single untolled run, `results/tolls.csv`, 500/590 links nonzero), applied only during the peak window. 15 paired seeds, tmax=14400.
- **Metric & baselines:** Paired difference in average delay (s) vs fixed_time, 95% t-CI. Also checked: whether the 15-seed delay distribution is bimodal (it was suspected at n=5).
- **Assumptions relied on:** A1-A6, A7, A10
- **Budget:** 15 seeds × ~2min = ~30 compute-min
- **Code & command:** `python scripts/compare.py` config=`pricing` (commit: "Price from an observed toll schedule; add combined-lever config")
- **Results:** `results/comparison.partial.csv` (config=pricing). n=15: delay 1580±91s vs baseline 2351±165s → **−31.89% ± 5.97%, 15/15 seeds negative.** Delay distribution: 1230, 1288, 1385, 1498, 1534, 1556, 1621, 1652, 1655, 1676, 1696, 1697, 1729, 1739, 1740 — largest gap is 112s (continuous, left-skewed, not bimodal).
- **Interpretation:** Matches the prediction for the main effect. The bimodality suspected from an earlier 5-seed run is **disproven** at 15 seeds — that was a small-sample artifact, not a real two-regime effect. H2 supported; no evidence of a routing-regime discontinuity.
- **Next:** This is a lower bound (A6 — fixed toll schedule, no demand elasticity). Iterating the toll to a fixed point, or adding elastic demand, would test whether the effect is understated.
