# E5 - Cordon (external) demand: does it expose the missing hotspots?

- **Status:** done
- **Owner:** human
- **Tests hypothesis:** none directly — this is a scope-extension check (A9), prerequisite to deciding whether H1-H3 should be re-run under cordon demand
- **Prediction (written BEFORE running):** If A9's cordon model is doing its job, we expect previously underweighted boundary-adjacent junctions (Kalanki, Chabahil) to rise sharply in the bottleneck ranking. We expect *some* completion/delay degradation from +24.7% more demand with no added capacity, but if the network fails to drain even at tmax=14400 that needs a cause (genuine overload vs. a stalled/looping tail) before trusting any number from this run.
- **Method:** `add_cordon.py` (7 of 8 compass sectors found a boundary node — one sector has none within the margin), then `run_baseline.py --zones-file data/zones_cordon.csv --od-file data/od_matrix_cordon.csv --tmax 14400`, then `find_bottlenecks.py` on the same files.
- **Metric & baselines:** completion %, mean speed, delay ratio, bottleneck concentration (top-20 share of delay) vs E1's internal-only baseline.
- **Assumptions relied on:** A9 (external-share=0.25, through-share=0.30, per-sector lane-count entry selection)
- **Budget:** 2 runs (baseline + bottleneck ranking), ~2-3 min each
- **Code & command:** `python scripts/add_cordon.py` → `python scripts/run_baseline.py --zones-file data/zones_cordon.csv --od-file data/od_matrix_cordon.csv --tmax 14400` → `python scripts/find_bottlenecks.py --zones-file data/zones_cordon.csv --od-file data/od_matrix_cordon.csv --tmax 14400 --top 15 --out data/bottlenecks_cordon.csv`
- **Results:** `results/baseline_cordon.log`, `data/bottlenecks_cordon.csv`. 79,615 trips loaded (64,244 external + internal share routed through the 7 cordon zones). Completion **84%** at tmax=14400 (vs ~100% internal-only), delay ratio 0.72 (vs 0.65-0.79 range seen before), mean speed 10.7 km/h (vs 13-14 km/h internal-only). Total delay **57,451 veh-h** (vs 27,361 internal-only — roughly doubled). Vehicle-in-network count plateaus flat at ~11,580-11,650 from t=10,800s to t=14,400s at near-zero average speed (0.2-0.6 m/s) — **not a slow drain, a stalled tail.** Bottleneck concentration flattened: top node now carries only 6.5% of delay (vs 22% on one node internal-only); top-20 carry 63.9% (vs 73% internal-only).
- **Interpretation:** **Prediction falsified on the specific claim it existed to test.** Demand degradation and flattened bottleneck concentration happened as expected (more, more-distributed load), but ran `validate_hotspots.py` against the cordon files and the landmark match got *worse*, not better: 2/7 → 1/7. Kalanki: V/C 0.27→0.18 (worse), delay 37.7s→66.3s. Chabahil: V/C 0.42→0.28 (worse), delay 16.8s→19.7s. Thapathali dropped out of the top-10 entirely.
  - **Root cause for Kalanki, diagnosed:** it sits only **1.25 km from the west edge of the OSM extract** (bbox starts at lon 85.268). The real feeder — the Thankot/Tribhuvan Highway corridor — is almost certainly outside the map entirely. No cordon-demand tuning can fix this; there is no link for that traffic to arrive on. This is a **map scope limitation**, not a calibration problem.
  - **Root cause for Chabahil, not yet diagnosed:** it's 3.65 km from the north edge (plausibly within real reach) and its nearest cordon node is only 2.7 km away, yet it still got worse. More likely a demand-weighting issue — A9's gravity-style split sends external trips to all 109 internal zones proportional to mass, rather than concentrating them on the actual corridor a real Chabahil-bound highway would feed. Unconfirmed.
  - The flat near-zero-speed tail from t=10,800-14,400s is still undiagnosed and is a separate open item from the landmark-matching failure above.
