# E7 - Sensitivity analysis: +/-20% on the baseline's 3 fitted knobs

- **Status:** done
- **Owner:** human
- **Tests hypothesis:** none directly — this checks how much the baseline's absolute numbers (speed, delay) move when its 3 fitted/assumed parameters (A2 PCU factor, A3 free-flow speed, A4 demand-scale) are each perturbed +/-20% alone, holding the other two at their calibrated value.
- **Prediction (written BEFORE running):** If the calibration is fragile, a +/-20% nudge on any one knob should move mean speed/delay by much more than 20% (the model's response is nonlinear near congestion onset — small demand changes have moved delay 2.3% before on just a 0.02% demand change, per the project's own documented seed-variance finding). If the calibration is reasonably robust, +/-20% on a knob should produce a roughly proportionate, not wildly amplified, change.
- **Method:** `scripts/sensitivity.py`, 3 seeds per variant, tmax=14400, fixed_time baseline only (not the H1-H3 lever comparisons — see Scope below for why).
- **Metric & baselines:** mean speed (km/h) and average delay (s), each knob's +/-20% variant vs. the calibrated baseline (3-seed mean each).
- **Assumptions relied on:** A2 (pcu_factor), A3 (free_flow_kmh), A4 (demand_scale) — this experiment directly stress-tests those three.
- **Budget:** 21 runs (3 knobs x 2 directions x 3 seeds + 3 baseline seeds, deduplicated), 3 at a time, ~10 min.
- **Code & command:** `python scripts/sensitivity.py --seeds 3 --jobs 3`
- **Results:** `results/sensitivity.csv`. Baseline (3 seeds): 13.69 km/h, 2151s delay.

| Knob | Variant | Speed (km/h) | % vs base | Delay (s) | % vs base | Completion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| demand_scale | -20% | 15.91 | +16.3% | 1314 | -38.9% | 100% (all seeds) |
| demand_scale | +20% | 10.08 | -26.4% | 2020 | -6.1% | **75.8-82.7%** |
| pcu_factor | -20% | 15.91 | +16.3% | 1314 | -38.9% | 100% (all seeds) |
| pcu_factor | +20% | 9.87 | -27.9% | 1906 | -11.4% | **74.5-81.4%** |
| free_flow_kmh | -20% | 10.31 | -24.7% | 2389 | +11.1% | **80.3-100%** (1 seed low, 2 high) |
| free_flow_kmh | +20% | 15.06 | +10.1% | 2113 | -1.8% | 100% (all seeds) |

- **Interpretation:**
  1. **The `-20%` rows for `demand_scale` and `pcu_factor` are bit-for-bit identical.** This is not a coincidence or a bug — it's a direct empirical confirmation of A4 (only the *product* of these two knobs is identified by the calibration). A clean, citable result: the paper can now *show*, not just argue, that the 0.48/0.625 split is unidentifiable.
  2. **The prediction is confirmed, but not the way "fragile vs robust" usually reads.** Every knob that increases congestion (`demand_scale`/`pcu_factor` +20%, `free_flow_kmh` -20%) drops completion well below 100% at tmax=14400 — down to 74.5% in the worst case, and with real seed-to-seed instability (`free_flow_kmh` -20% ranges 80.3-100% across 3 seeds). **This reproduces the project's own previously-documented survivorship-bias trap**, now showing up in the sensitivity analysis itself: `demand_scale +20%`'s "-6.1% delay" is not "more demand causes less delay" — it's fewer trips finishing, so the average among survivors looks artificially better. Any of these three congestion-increasing variants' delay numbers should be read with that caveat, not taken at face value.
  3. **Net finding:** the baseline is not comfortably inside a stable operating regime — it sits close enough to a capacity cliff that a +20% nudge on any one of its three fitted knobs pushes a meaningful share of trips into non-completion even at the already-extended tmax=14400. That is itself the sensitivity answer, and it's a real limitation worth stating plainly rather than quoting the raw delay percentages as if they were comparable to the 100%-completion baseline.
- **Next:** Report both findings (1: clean A4 confirmation; 2: the capacity-cliff/survivorship caveat) in the paper's limitations section. Not pursuing the full 4-config x 6-variant sweep (84 runs) given this already answers the scoped question and surfaces a real caveat that would apply to any extension of it too.

## Scope note: baseline only, not the full H1-H3 sweep

A full sensitivity sweep would re-run all 4 configs (fixed_time/adaptive/pricing/adaptive_pricing) under each +/-20% variant — 4x the cost of this card (84 runs instead of 21) for a question (`deadlines.md`'s own wording: "sensitivity analysis (+/-20% on demand/PCU/speed)") that's really about the baseline calibration's fragility, not whether the already-established, 15-seed-paired H1-H3 results would change direction. If this card finds the baseline is fragile under +/-20%, that's grounds to consider extending to the full sweep before writing; if it's reasonably robust, the smaller check is sufficient and the extra 4x cost isn't needed.
