# Decision log

Append-only. Each decision records its **dissent**: the strongest counterargument that was not refuted. Dissent is kept, not deleted, so we know what to revisit.

## D1 - Represent lane indiscipline as reduced free-flow speed, not driver behavior (2026-10-13)
- **Decision:** Override every link's free-flow speed to 30 km/h (vs OSM's 50 km/h tag) as a single friction factor (α=0.60) standing in for roadside parking, informal stops, encroachment and poor lane discipline.
- **Why:** UXsim is mesoscopic — no car-following model, no reaction time, no lane-level dynamics — so these effects cannot be represented as behavior.
- **Alternatives considered:** Build a microscopic model (SUMO) with car-following and reaction time instead.
- **Dissent:** The model reproduces the *outcome* of poor driving conditions but cannot test whether driver behavior is the *cause*, or distinguish it from e.g. intersection geometry. Any claim about *why* speeds are low is unsupported.
- **Revisit if:** A SUMO/microscopic companion model is built, or Kathmandu-specific speed-flow survey data becomes available.
- **Decided by:** human

## D2 - Use within-day DUO, not day-to-day DUE, as the route-choice process (2026-10-15)
- **Decision:** Results use UXsim's default `homogeneous_DUO` (rerouting on current travel time every 600s). `SolverDUE` was attempted and abandoned.
- **Why:** `SolverDUE` oscillates rather than converging on this network (route-cost gap ran 611.6 → 13.5 → 510.0 → 36.5 over 4 iterations; up to 39,355/55,900 vehicles failed to finish in a bad iteration).
- **Alternatives considered:** More DUE iterations / damping; a different route-enumeration scheme.
- **Dissent:** The oscillation might reflect genuinely unstable or multiple equilibria on this network rather than a solver tuning problem — in which case no amount of damping fixes it, and the within-day DUO result is not simply "DUE's limit as it converges."
- **Revisit if:** A different route-enumeration or damped DUE scheme is tried and converges, or the instability is diagnosed as structural.
- **Decided by:** human

## D3 - Calibrate "30% of citywide demand" against mean speed only, decomposed as 0.48 arterial share × 0.625 PCU (2026-10-15)
- **Decision:** Load 30% of the full gravity-model matrix onto the arterial-only network; treat this as 0.48 (share of trips plausibly on arterials) × 0.625 (PCU weighting).
- **Why:** The network only contains trunk/primary/secondary roads — loading 100% of demand gridlocks it (12% completion, 3 km/h). 30% was tuned so mean speed matches the observed Kathmandu peak-hour range.
- **Alternatives considered:** Survey-based arterial/local split; fit PCU and arterial share independently against two targets.
- **Dissent:** Only the *product* is identified by a single calibration target (mean speed) — the 0.48/0.625 split is a defensible decomposition, not a measured one. A different split with the same product gives identical simulation results but a different story about *why*.
- **Revisit if:** Surveyed OD data, a second independent calibration target (e.g. observed PCU mix from a count survey), or GEH validation against link counts becomes available.
- **Decided by:** human

## D4 - Cap congestion pricing at 0.2× observed per-link delay, measured from a single untolled run (2026-10-15)
- **Decision:** `pricing`/`adaptive_pricing` configs toll each link at 0.2× its observed delay (seconds), from a schedule measured once, applied only during the peak demand window.
- **Why:** The worst link shows 3,733s of observed delay — charging it in full (1.0×) would price the corridor out of existence and measure a road closure, not a toll.
- **Alternatives considered:** Full-cost (1.0×) marginal pricing; iterating the toll schedule to convergence (two-stage: observe, price, re-observe, re-price).
- **Dissent:** A fixed, once-measured schedule with no demand elasticity to price (drivers reroute but don't cancel trips) makes every pricing result a **lower bound** — a real deployed toll would plausibly suppress some demand too, for a larger effect than reported here.
- **Revisit if:** Demand elasticity is added, or the toll schedule is iterated to a fixed point.
- **Decided by:** human

## D5 - Signalise intersections by widest angular gap between approach axes, ≥3 approaches, ≥30° spread (2026-10-13)
- **Decision:** 128 of 233 intersections get a fixed-time 2-phase signal; 105 stay as free merges (bends, diverges, two-way merges).
- **Why:** UXsim's OSM import makes every node an unsignalised free merge. A stricter rule (3+ approaches within 45°) skipped a 3-into-1 merge carrying 15.7% of all delay; a looser rule (2+ approaches, 165 signals) over-constrained the network (78% completion, 11.5 km/h) — total delay looked lower only because the 22% of trips that never finished weren't counted.
- **Alternatives considered:** The two rejected thresholds above; using OSM's own (nonexistent, for this import) signal tags.
- **Dissent:** No turn-movement or real signal-timing-plan data exists to validate this placement against. "Controllable" in the bottleneck ranking means "this heuristic signalised it," not "Kathmandu Traffic Police actually runs a signal here."
- **Revisit if:** Real signal-timing plans or turn-count data are obtained from the Department of Roads / Traffic Police.
- **Decided by:** human

## D6 - Add cordon (external) demand at 25% of internal volume, entry points = highest-lane-count node per compass sector (2026-10-17)
- **Decision:** Ran `add_cordon.py` and the baseline against it: 64,244 external trips (24.7% of internal) over 7 entry points (only 7 of 8 sectors had a boundary node). Result: completion drops to 84% at tmax=14400 (vs ~100% without cordon), delay roughly doubles (27,361 → 57,451 veh-h), and the bottleneck distribution flattens (previously the single dominant node at 22% of delay no longer dominates).
- **Why:** The internal-only matrix made Kalanki and Chabahil — real Kathmandu hotspots — rank 138th/131st of 233, since all their congestion in reality comes from outside the study box.
- **Alternatives considered:** No cordon (status quo); a global top-N boundary selection (rejected earlier — it clustered all entry points in the north/west due to tied lane counts).
- **Dissent:** 25% external-share and 30% through-share are both untested guesses (`[SURVEY]` tag in the script), not measured. **Confirmed after running the hotspot check (E5):** this did not achieve its purpose. Face-validity against named landmarks got *worse* (2/7 → 1/7), not better. Kalanki is only 1.25 km from the west edge of the OSM extract — the real Thankot/Tribhuvan Highway feeder is outside the map entirely, so no cordon-demand parameter can fix it; that needs a wider OSM re-extraction, not a re-tune. Chabahil's regression is unexplained (it's geometrically within reach of a cordon node) and may be a demand-weighting issue in `add_cordon.py`'s mass-proportional allocation. The 84% completion at tmax=14400 also showed a flat, near-zero-speed tail from t=10,800-14,400s, still undiagnosed as genuine overload vs. an artifact.
- **Revisit if:** A real cordon count (Thankot, Bhaktapur road, etc.) is obtained; the OSM bounding box is widened to include Thankot; or the stalled-tail and Chabahil-regression questions above are resolved. Until then, treat cordon demand as **not working as intended** — don't fold it into H1-H3's main results.
- **Decided by:** human

## D8 - First GEH results are mixed: Thapathali fits, Koteshwor badly undershoots (2026-10-17)
- **Decision:** Treat Koteshwor's GEH 102.22 (model 2,060 pcu/h vs observed 9,996 pcu/h) as a real finding to investigate, not noise to average away. Thapathali's GEH 0.77 is a genuine good fit on the same calibration.
- **Why:** Node-matching for Koteshwor is precise (0.03 km), ruling out a landmark-mismatch artifact. One good and one bad result on two points, from a single mean-speed calibration, is exactly what you'd expect if the calibration (A3/A4) fixes total demand level but leaves spatial distribution unconstrained.
- **Alternatives considered:** Dismissing Koteshwor as within acceptable GEH variance (rejected — GEH 102 is two orders of magnitude over the GEH<5 threshold, not a borderline case); re-tuning demand-scale specifically to fix Koteshwor (rejected for now — would overfit one point and could break Thapathali's current good fit).
- **Dissent (checked and resolved):** It's also possible Koteshwor is another instance of the already-known external/cordon-demand gap (E5/D6), like Kalanki. **Ruled out directly:** Koteshwor is 4.22-4.88 km from the nearest OSM bounding-box edges (vs. Kalanki's 1.25 km) — not a boundary case. Also ruled out missing network geometry: the matched node has 7 links including both Araniko Highway and Kathmandu Ring Road, 4 inbound approaches, correctly representing it as a major convergence point. The remaining explanation is the calibration's spatial-distribution gap (A4), not a second instance of D6 or a topology bug.
- **Revisit if:** More landmarks are checked (if undershoots cluster specifically at major multi-corridor convergence points regardless of position in the map, that would confirm the calibration explanation over a boundary-position explanation).
- **Decided by:** human

## D9 - GEH validation fails at intersection level (2/6, 33%), confirmed as systematic, not one outlier (2026-10-17)
- **Decision:** Treat the extended 6-landmark GEH result as a real, reportable validation failure, not a data-coverage gap to keep patching. Only Thapathali (0.77) and Old Baneshwor (3.16) pass GEH<5; Koteshwor (102.22, undershoot), Kalimati (17.61, undershoot), New Baneshwor (24.28, undershoot), and Sorakhutte (16.13, **overshoot**) all fail.
- **Why:** Sorakhutte overshooting while Koteshwor/Kalimati/New Baneshwor undershoot rules out a single directional story ("big junctions always undershoot"). Coordinate precision doesn't explain it either — the 2 surveyed-precision landmarks split 1-good/1-bad, same ratio as the 4 approximate ones. The simplest remaining explanation is the one already on record (A4): a single city-wide mean-speed calibration constrains total demand, not its spatial distribution, and this result shows that gap is large in practice.
- **Alternatives considered:** Attempting a second-stage, multi-point calibration (deferred — real effort, risk of overfitting to exactly these 6 points, not undertaken without the human's go-ahead given deadline pressure); dismissing failing points as geocoding error (rejected — Thapathali and Koteshwor both use precise surveyed coordinates and still split 1 good/1 bad, so imprecision isn't the deciding factor).
- **Dissent:** With only 6 points (4 of them approximately geocoded), this is still a small sample — a different 6 landmarks could show a different pass rate. The *qualitative* conclusion (single aggregate calibration doesn't guarantee intersection-level fit) is solid; the *exact* 33% figure should not be over-quoted as precise.
- **Revisit if:** A second-stage spatial calibration is attempted and tested against these same 6 points; or more landmarks are transcribed and the pass rate changes materially.
- **Decided by:** human

## D14 - Drop pricing from the hackathon deliverable, keep it for the paper (2026-10-18)
- **Decision:** The hackathon's build/pitch scope is now signal optimization only, plus the pending time-based one-way/tidal-flow idea. Congestion pricing (H2/H3's results) stays in the project's evidence base for the later academic paper, but is no longer something to prototype or present to hackathon judges.
- **Why:** Checked comparable-country evidence for congestion pricing specifically (not the wealthy-city examples - London/Singapore/Stockholm - that don't resemble Nepal's institutional capacity). Found no well-documented case of pricing being implemented and measured in a low/middle-income country. Jakarta's electronic road pricing proposal never launched ("stuck in bureaucratic deadlock" for over a decade per reporting). Its actual substitute, the 3-in-1 occupancy restriction, was defeated by hired "jockeys" (paid strangers, sometimes children, faking passenger counts to pass manual police checks) - a direct, concrete example of a human-enforced demand-management rule failing under exactly the kind of enforcement capacity constraints Nepal would also face. Plate-based restrictions elsewhere (Lima) showed congestion gains disappearing within 16 weeks via spillover/workarounds. Signal retiming has none of this exposure - no payment collection, no behavioral compliance needed from the public, pure backend software/hardware.
- **Alternatives considered:** Keeping pricing in the pitch but reframed as "phase-2/aspirational" (considered, but the human decided to drop it entirely from the hackathon scope rather than hedge); abandoning the pricing research entirely including from the paper (rejected - it's valid, already-run evidence, no reason to discard it just because this specific deliverable doesn't use it).
- **Dissent:** This is a judgment call about THIS hackathon and THIS implementation context, not a claim that congestion pricing can never work in Nepal. Automated, camera/tag-based pricing (removing the human-discretion failure point entirely) might avoid the jockey-style gaming problem - but that infrastructure doesn't exist yet, making it correctly out of scope for a 6-month pilot regardless.
- **Revisit if:** The human wants pricing back in for a later phase once automated enforcement infrastructure is actually available, or for the paper's discussion of what a full-scope deployment would need.
- **Decided by:** human

## D16 - H4 (tidal-flow lever) rejected as implemented: too-aggressive capacity shift causes new gridlock (2026-10-18)
- **Decision:** The tidal-flow lever (3 corridors, 1.0 lane-equivalent shift from light to heavy direction, AM-biased demand) does not help. Of 15 paired seeds, 5 that completed 100% without the lever dropped to 75.6-81.5% completion with it - the lever itself creates new gridlock on the lightened direction. Among the 7 seeds unaffected by this (100% completion both ways), the lever makes delay *worse* on average (+37.1s, 3/7 improved). The raw paired result (-9.53%, not significant) was itself a survivorship-bias artifact driven by the 5 newly-gridlocked seeds, not a real effect either direction.
- **Why:** Checked completion before trusting the raw delay comparison, per this project's own established practice (D11, the original signal-placement trap) - exactly the right call, since the raw number would have been actively misleading (looked like a modest improvement; was actually a mix of "slightly worse" and "caused new gridlock").
- **Alternatives considered:** Re-running with a smaller lane_shift (0.5 instead of 1.0) immediately (deferred to "Next" in E8.md rather than done automatically - a parameter change deserves its own prediction, not a silent retry); checking each corridor's light-direction baseline capacity before shifting (same - a real next step, not done here).
- **Dissent:** This rejects the specific implementation (3 corridors, full 1-lane shift, 1.4x demand skew), not the tidal-flow concept in general. A gentler shift or better-chosen corridors could behave differently - that's untested, not disproven.
- **Revisit if:** A smaller lane_shift or capacity-aware corridor selection is tried.
- **Decided by:** human

## D19 - H6 (staggered departures) supported: the strongest single-lever result in this project (2026-10-18)

- **Dissent:** Unlike signal optimization, this needs behavior change from many institutions (schools, offices) to coordinate - a different, non-trivial adoption risk, even though it needs no new technology.
- **Revisit if:** A smaller, more realistic stagger window is tested.

## D21 - MAJOR CORRECTION: H3 (super-additivity) is falsified, not supported - the project's central claim since E4 was wrong (2026-10-19)
- **Decision:** H3 ("combining adaptive signals and pricing reduces delay by more than the sum of their individual effects") is **falsified by its own stated criterion**. The correct per-seed interaction test - combined_diff minus (adaptive_diff + pricing_diff), computed properly for the first time from `results/comparison.partial.csv` - gives **-185.3 +/- 275.6 seconds (t=-1.44, df=14), CI includes zero, 9/15 seeds negative (not a clean majority)**. This replaces the original E4.md interpretation, which compared the combined effect's point estimate (-65.30%) against a "-48.6% independence prediction" that has no reproducible derivation: neither a naive sum of the two mean percentages (-56.39%) nor a per-seed-paired sum (also -56.39%) produces -48.6%, and the actual statistical test specified in E4's own "Metric & baselines" line was apparently never run. The combined lever's own effect (-65.30% +/- 4.47% vs. fixed-time, 15/15 negative) is real, strong, and entirely unaffected by this correction - only the claim that it exceeds independent/additive effects is wrong.
- **Why:** Caught during a `qa-check` audit of the paper draft (`lab/qa/2026-10-19-qa.md`, finding P1), specifically because drafting the paper required generating Table 1 from real result files and writing out the methodology sentence-by-sentence, which surfaced that the "-48.6%" figure could not be reproduced. This is exactly the audit step the paper-draft skill requires before finalizing - it worked as intended, just later than ideal (this error had already propagated into the hackathon writeup doc and pitch deck before being caught).
- **Alternatives considered:** Re-deriving a different "independence prediction" methodology that might happen to produce something closer to the original framing (rejected - the correct test is the one H3's own falsification criterion specifies; inventing a different comparison after the fact to preserve a conclusion is exactly the kind of result-shopping this project's statistical discipline exists to prevent).
- **Dissent:** None of substance on the arithmetic (independently verified seed-by-seed, by hand, against `scripts/compare.py`'s own `ci95()`). It remains possible that a larger seed count would show a real but smaller interaction effect reaching significance - 15 seeds with this much seed-to-seed variance (interaction ranging from -958 to +718 seconds) is not strong evidence of absence, only an honest "not detected at this n."
- **Revisit if:** More seeds are run specifically on the interaction term (not the individual configs, which are already well-powered) to check whether a smaller, non-zero effect would resolve with more data.
- **Corrected downstream:** `lab/charter.md` (H3 status, "What success looks like"), `lab/experiments/E4.md` (results/interpretation), `lab/paper/claims.md` (C3 split - combined-effect magnitude stays strong, super-additivity claim removed), the paper draft (`paper/sections/*.tex`), and the hackathon writeup doc + pitch deck (both claimed "more than additive" in places) - all corrected in this same pass. The hackathon deck/doc are being de-prioritized per the human's direction (judges will see the GitHub repo directly, not the pitch materials), but are still being corrected for accuracy since they remain published, shareable artifacts.
- **Decided by:** human (directed the fix); error found and corrected by the assistant during the paper-draft audit step.

## D22 - Simulation realism refinement: surface density, Ring Road bridges, and jam chain-reaction (2026-10-19)
- **Decision:** Add `scripts/find_bridges.py` (recovers the OSM `bridge` tag, which UXsim's importer discards, by re-parsing the raw `data/kathmandu.osm.xml` and snapping bridge-way endpoints to the already-merged network's nearest surviving nodes) and `scripts/congestion_report.py` (per-link density via UXsim's own Edie's-generalized-definitions output, `Analyzer.link_traffic_state_to_pandas`, normalized by each link's actual jam capacity `l.kappa`, not the misleadingly flat `l.jam_density` attribute). Density, queue spillback and bottleneck throttling are not new mechanisms: UXsim's kinematic-wave link model already represents them physically (a saturated link throttles its upstream neighbours' inflow). This work surfaces and reports that existing physics at specific real locations (named bridges), not adds it.
- **Why:** The human asked for the simulation to reflect more real-life variables: density, location-specific jams, Ring Road bridge bottlenecks, and chain-reaction propagation between locations - all physically present already but never reported at link/location granularity.
- **Finding:** Of 71 bridge ways tagged in the raw OSM extract on trunk/primary/secondary roads, 13 resolve to a distinct link in the simulated (merged) network, 2 of them on the Ring Road; 18 were fully absorbed into a single intersection node by the 55 m node-merge step (the bridge no longer exists as a distinct link - it is zero-length, internal to a junction); 40 could not be snapped within 150 m of any surviving node (likely outside osmnx's live-queried area/filter, which is a separate extraction from the static XML file).
- **Alternatives considered:** Exact OSM node-ID matching (tried first, matched only 1/112 pairs - the merge step renumbers/discards most short-span endpoints, so this was abandoned as unusably lossy, not just imprecise).
- **Dissent:** The 40 unresolved bridges and 18 absorbed ones mean this bottleneck analysis covers only a minority of the area's real bridges - real locations like these (Bagmati, Bishnumati, Sinamangal, Manohara) are captured, but the list is not exhaustive, and nothing should be claimed about a bridge not in `data/bridges.csv`.
- **Revisit if:** A higher-resolution network (smaller merge threshold, more node preserved) is built, or the live osmnx query and the static XML extract are reconciled to recover the 40 unresolved matches.
- **Decided by:** human (requested the refinement); the severity-threshold judgment call in `congestion_report.py`'s `classify_severity()` is left to the human per this project's "Learn by Doing" convention for business-logic design decisions.

## D23 - Two principled GEH-fit hypotheses tested, both rejected: fit stays 2/6 (2026-10-19)
- **Decision:** Per the human's direction, tested two pre-specified (not reverse-engineered from GEH) hypotheses for why 4/6 landmarks fail GEH: (1) a cold-start warmup of 3600s, derived from network geometry (bbox diagonal ~17.7km at 30km/h free-flow, scaled by the baseline's own calibrated delay ratio ~1.65x) so downstream nodes aren't measured before traffic has reached them; (2) lane-weighted zone mass in the demand model (`scripts/build_od.py`), so a junction where more lanes converge counts as a bigger trip generator than raw junction count, decided from road capacity alone. Tested each in isolation and combined. **Both made the fit worse, not better**: warmup alone broke the previously-passing Thapathali (GEH 0.77 -> 18.28) while only partly helping Koteshwor (102.22 -> 75.56, still failing); lane-weighting alone also broke Thapathali (-> 14.13) and worsened Kalimati, New Baneshwor and Sorakhutte, for a similarly small Koteshwor gain (-> 77.05). Combined: 1/6 pass, down from the published 2/6. Reverted both; `results/geh.csv` and every file depending on it (the paper, hackathon materials) are unchanged and still reflect the originally published, correct numbers.
- **Why:** Honest testing of plausible, principled fixes, not circular tuning against the validation set (per the dissent recorded when this was proposed - see the question raised before this test, and D21's precedent for what result-shopping looks like in this project). Both hypotheses were reasonable a priori; both turned out to be wrong, which is itself informative: Thapathali's good fit depends specifically on measuring from t=0 with junction-count-based demand, which means its fit may be partly coincidental (right node, right window, for reasons not fully understood) rather than a sign the model is broadly correct nearby.
- **Alternatives considered:** Continuing to adjust (different warmup lengths, different lane-weight formulas, trying only on specific nodes) until something passed - explicitly rejected as circular; stopped at the first clean negative result on both hypotheses instead.
- **Dissent:** None of substance on the arithmetic (both isolated and combined runs reproduced consistently). It remains possible that a different, more targeted structural fix (e.g. a genuinely through-traffic-specific demand term, not lane-count as a proxy for it) would work where these two did not - not tested here.
- **Revisit if:** A through-traffic-specific demand mechanism (e.g. explicit external/pass-through trips assigned directly between Ring Road and highway entry points, rather than via the gravity model's zone-to-zone trips) is designed and tested the same way: decided before checking GEH, reported honestly either way.
- **Decided by:** human (requested both tests after being warned of the circularity risk); negative result found, reverted, and logged by the assistant.
